Temporal-network findings

The observations below answer the five assignment questions using the animated view and summaries calculated from all 60 days.

1

How does overall connectivity change?

The network becomes denser late in the period. Days 1–20 average 5.50 active links and 8.55 active companies, while days 41–60 average 7.25 links and 9.65 active companies. Daily value rises from about $77,196 to $144,326.

2

Which companies become central or active?

Fusion Electronics has the greatest total 60-day volume (about $1.99M) and eight distinct partners. In days 31–60, Ion Systems becomes the volume leader (about $1.17M), followed by Fusion Electronics and Cedar Retail.

3

When does the network separate into clusters?

Fragmentation is strongest on low-activity days such as 1, 7, 11, 13, 17, and 19: each has only three links and several isolated pairs. The later period has fewer components on average (5.05 including isolates, versus 6.50 earlier), so clusters are increasingly bridged.

4

Which relationships change or become stronger?

Links appear and disappear from day to day rather than remaining continuously active. The strongest repeated relationship is Delta Components–Fusion Electronics (about $440K across 20 active days). Other persistent high-value pairs include Ion Systems–Lumen Distribution and Apex Textiles–BlueRiver Logistics.

5

Does the network become more cross-regional?

Yes. Cross-regional links account for an average of 55.7% of daily links in days 1–20, 63.0% in days 21–40, and 72.5% in days 41–60. The animation therefore shows a clear shift from more regionally contained activity toward a more internationally connected network.
