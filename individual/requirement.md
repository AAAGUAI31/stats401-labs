Overview
In this individual project, you will select existing data visualizations, such as a dashboard containing multiple visualizations or a complex single visualization, critically evaluate its design, and create an improved version using D3.js.

The goal is to demonstrate that effective visualization involves more than producing a technically correct chart. You should be able to identify what a visualization is trying to communicate, evaluate its strengths and weaknesses, and make justified design changes that improve clarity and effectiveness.

Learning Objectives
By completing this project, you should be able to:

identify the intended message, audience, and tasks of a visualization;
evaluate how effectively the visualization communicates its data;
identify specific design strengths and weaknesses;
propose improvements based on visualization principles;
implement a redesigned visualization using D3.js;
justify how your redesign improves the original.
Part 1. Select a Visualization
Choose an existing visualization from a news article, report, paper, dashboard, public website, social media post, or another public source.

Include:

the original visualization or a screenshot;
title and source;
link to the original source when available;
a brief description of the data being shown.
Choose a visualization with enough complexity to support meaningful critique. For example, you may select a dashboard containing multiple coordinated visualizations or a complex single visualization. Ideally, the selected visualization should contain both strengths and opportunities for improvement.

Part 2. Analyze the Original Visualization
Briefly explain:

Intended Message: What is the visualization trying to communicate? What important trends, comparisons, relationships, or patterns should viewers notice?

Audience: Who is the intended audience?

Tasks: What should viewers be able to do with the visualization, such as compare values, identify trends, locate outliers, explore patterns, or understand relationships?

Part 3. Critique the Visualization
Identify at least:

two strengths, and
three weaknesses or opportunities for improvement.
Consider aspects such as:

choice of visual marks and channels;
position, length, size, color, and shape;
scales and axes;
labels and legends;
visual hierarchy;
clutter and readability;
effectiveness of comparisons;
suitability for the intended message, audience, and tasks.
For each weakness, explain what the problem is, why it matters, and how it could be improved. Avoid purely subjective comments such as “the chart looks bad.”

Part 4. Redesign
Create an improved version using D3.js.

You may keep the original visualization type or choose a different technique. You may also modify the layout, visual encodings, labels, colors, organization, or interactions.

Your redesign should directly address the problems identified in your critique.

Use an external dataset such as CSV or JSON. You may use the original dataset, reconstruct the data when appropriate, or use an equivalent dataset from a reliable source.

Interactions such as tooltips, filtering, highlighting, or sorting are optional and should only be included when they support meaningful visualization tasks.

Part 5. Explain Your Design
Describe at least three important redesign decisions.

For each decision, briefly explain:

what you changed;
why you changed it;
which problem it addresses; and
how it improves the visualization.
Finally, compare the original and redesigned versions. Explain what information is easier to understand or what tasks are easier to perform after the redesign. You should also briefly discuss any important trade-offs or limitations.

Deliverables
Submit your project through your GitHub Pages course website under a tab or section titled Visualization Critique and Redesign, which shows two parts:

1. GitHub Pages Visualization
The page should include:

original visualization and source;
brief introduction to the visualization and data;
your redesigned D3.js visualization;
data source and references.
2. Visualization Critique and Redesign Report
a 500–800 word report documenting your critique and redesign process.

The report should include:

Original visualization and context: Introduce the visualization, data source, intended message, audience, and tasks.
Critique: Discuss at least two strengths and three weaknesses using visualization principles.
Redesign rationale: Explain at least three major redesign decisions and how they address weaknesses identified in your critique.
Original vs. redesign: Explain what your redesign improves and identify any remaining limitations or trade-offs.
Figures and references: Include screenshots of the original and redesigned visualizations and cite the visualization and data sources.
The report should focus primarily on visualization reasoning and design decisions, rather than explaining your D3.js code.
