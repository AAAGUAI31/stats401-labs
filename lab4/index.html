What the comparison shows

This project uses Customer Support on Twitter, a public corpus of exchanges between consumers and company support accounts. I selected a reproducible, balanced sample of 5,000 tweets: 2,500 inbound customer messages and 2,500 outbound support replies. The cleaning script checks missing values and data types, removes duplicate IDs and unusable text or dates, standardizes whitespace, parses timestamps, and converts the inbound field into Customer and Support categories. Usernames and URLs are lightly normalized before every message is evaluated by the RoBERTa model. Sentiment score is defined as the positive probability minus the negative probability. The chart uses one 100% stacked bar per author type; segment length represents the within-group share predicted Negative, Neutral, or Positive. Negative is the largest customer category (49.2%), while Neutral is largest among support replies (58.2%). Support has a higher mean sentiment score (+0.160) than customers (−0.267). These outputs are model-generated estimates rather than ground-truth labels, so sarcasm, ambiguity, slang, and scripted support language can still be misclassified.
