4. Semantic findings

These observations are calculated from the cleaned passages, topic assignments, original embeddings, and coordinated views.

1

What are the major semantic topics?

The largest topic is Global Society, History and Politics (283 passages), followed by STEM, Data and Systems (166). The remaining themes cover language and writing (133), interdisciplinary majors (123), student services (114), credit and degree rules (92), enrollment progress (68), and leave or health policies (36).

2

Which topics cross formal sections?

Student Services and Administration appears in 37 sections, credit and degree rules in 28, enrollment and term progress in 22, and interdisciplinary research in 20. Formal boundaries therefore do not isolate these concerns; advising, eligibility, progression, and support recur throughout the bulletin.

3

Which sections are semantically diverse?

Course Descriptions spans seven topics. Among more compact sections, Major Requirements has six topics and the highest entropy (2.06 bits), while Degree Requirements covers five topics (2.03 bits). Study Away also crosses four topics, connecting scheduling, services, credits, and global learning.

4

Which passages from different sections are similar?

Passages p0122 (Transfer of Work Taken Elsewhere) and p0300 (Office of Global Education and Study Away) have cosine similarity 0.991: both explain approval and transfer of outside credit. Parallel warning policies for older and newer class cohorts also form nearly identical cross-section pairs.

5

Which passages are unusual for their section?

Within Major Requirements, p0336 and p0337 discuss EAP and third-language obligations and have section-centroid similarities of only 0.139 and 0.239. Their language-policy meaning differs from the section’s dominant major-curriculum content, making them visible semantic outliers.

6

Does “credit” occupy one semantic region?

No. Credit occurs in 132 passages across all eight topics: 56 concern grades and degree progress, but it also appears in enrollment, language, leave, major, service, STEM, and global-society contexts. Graduation spans four topics; registration spans four; academic integrity appears in two.

5. Design description

Methods and visual design (247 words)

The 400-page 2021-2022 DKU Undergraduate Bulletin was downloaded from the official university admissions site. PDF font size and weight identified Parts, sections, subsections, and course titles. Pages 3-9 (the table of contents), page numbers, repeated text, grade tables, short fragments, and course-list rows were removed. Long blocks were split at sentence boundaries, leaving 1,015 interpretable passages from 1,314 raw blocks. Each passage retains chapter, section, subsection, PDF page, original text, and word count.

For a fully local and reproducible semantic model, normalized TF-IDF vectors were projected into a 16-dimensional latent semantic analysis (LSA) space with truncated singular-value power iteration. PCA then reduced the normalized LSA embeddings to two dimensions. PCA was chosen as the justified alternative to UMAP because it is deterministic, exposes broad corpus structure, and requires no externally downloaded model. Cosine K-means with eight clusters was fitted in the original 16-dimensional space, not the display coordinates. Topic names were assigned only after inspecting characteristic TF-IDF terms and three centroid-nearest passages per cluster.

In the map, position represents projected semantic similarity, fill represents topic, and radius represents passage length. Search and section/topic filters alter opacity without removing context; zoom and pan support dense regions. Clicking a passage shows its full hierarchy and five nearest neighbors calculated in the original embedding space. The matrix aggregates count and within-section proportion for every section-topic pair. Matrix clicks highlight matching map points, while point clicks outline the corresponding cell, directly connecting formal structure with semantic structure.
