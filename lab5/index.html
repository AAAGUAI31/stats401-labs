1. Node-Link Visualization

Drag stations to adjust the layout. Hover over a station or route to view details and highlight related connections.

Node-Link Design Description

Node color distinguishes districts, making cross-district connections easy to see. Node area represents daily passenger volume, while circles, squares, and triangles identify Local, Transfer, and Terminal stations without relying on color alone. Route color distinguishes Metro, Express, and Shuttle service, and line width represents direct travel time, with wider edges indicating longer trips. Labels identify stations, while dragging, neighbor highlighting, and tooltips reduce clutter when all 50 nodes are visible.

2. Adjacency Matrix

Hover over a cell to highlight its row and column and inspect connection, route type, and travel-time details.

Matrix Design Description

Rows and columns both represent stations, and a colored cell indicates a direct connection. Cell color preserves route type, while opacity represents travel time. Stations are ordered first by district and then by station type, so each district forms a continuous block; axis-label colors also encode district. This ordering helps reveal within- and cross-district links, although paths are less intuitive than in the node-link view. Hover highlighting supports precise lookup of individual station pairs.

Each finding is based on the visual encodings and interactions above. The rating also indicates how well the selected view supports the question.

Question Answerability and View Visual Evidence and Observation
1. Which stations appear central in the network? Well · Both views Node positions and hover highlighting, together with filled matrix cells, show that s4, s7, s10, s13, s16, s18, s21, s23, s25, s29, s31, s33, s34, and s38 each have three direct links—the highest degree in this network.
2. Which districts are strongly connected? Well · Adjacency matrix District-ordered blocks show that South–East is the densest pairing with 11 direct links. Central–North and East–West follow with nine links each.
3. Where are Transfer and Terminal stations located? Partially · Node-link Node shapes show that several Terminal stations sit at shortcut endpoints and form degree-three hubs, including s4, s7, s10, s13, s16, s25, s30,s33 and s34. Transfer stations are more dispersed and include the outer branch endpoints s41, s44, s47, and s50.
4. Which stations have high passenger volume? Well · Node-link Node area shows that s46–s50 are the five largest stations, with s50 the largest. High volume does not necessarily imply topological centrality: s46, s48, and s49 have no direct links.
5. Where are the longest direct connections? Well · Both views The widest edges and most opaque matrix cells identify s7–s31 as the longest connection at 16 minutes, followed by s18–s34 at 15 minutes and s29–s50 at 14 minutes.
6. Are route types concentrated in particular areas? Partially · Both views Route colors alternate along the main chain and cross-district shortcuts, with no strong local concentration. Counts are also nearly balanced: 17 Express, 17 Shuttle, and 16 Metro links.
