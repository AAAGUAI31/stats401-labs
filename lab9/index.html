3. Design and Comparison

In the choropleth, the United States and China receive the darkest colors because their GDP values are far above the rest. The logarithmic color scale is appropriate because the dataset ranges from roughly $300 billion to more than $30 trillion; a linear scale would make most economies look nearly identical. Large countries such as Canada, Russia, and Australia still occupy substantial screen space, however, so geographic size can compete with the color signal.

The cartogram makes economic magnitude more immediate. The United States and China expand dramatically, while Germany, Japan, India, the United Kingdom, and France also become prominent. Canada, Russia, and Australia shrink relative to their land area, whereas geographically small economies such as Singapore and Hong Kong become easier to notice. The choropleth preserves familiar boundaries, locations, and neighborhood relationships, making it better for finding regional patterns and identifying where high-GDP economies are located. The cartogram sacrifices shape, distance, and geographic area, but it better supports comparisons of total economic size and concentration. Moving from color to area changes the emphasis: color communicates an ordered spatial pattern, while area makes the imbalance in global output feel physical. Neither view is universally better; each supports a different analytical task, and linked highlighting helps users match distorted countries to their original locations.
